one invariant, only one of them written. Attempt 4 wrote **`Match.nextPlayer` instead**, placed no
pins, and submitted six alternating moves through **T1j's own `Match.setlastMove()`**, letting the
engine populate both boards, the move list, `moveNr` and player state **together**. The position is
coherent by construction rather than by injection.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Established, under the qualified runtime (Temurin 17.0.20.1, isolated Preferences,
`java.awt.headless=true`):** T1j can be loaded from its official release JAR, initialized via its
two public routines, driven to a coherent six-move position through its own move-application path,
and made to return **one legal move at fixed ply 3 with alpha-beta completing** — with GUI classes
resident, **no window instantiated**, the host preference surfaces untouched, and exactly one
reflective write plus two reflective reads.

**The claim stays where the authorization put it: headless execution via narrowly scoped
private-state injection — not a published headless API.** One reflective write of a private field
is still required, because `nextPlayer` is set only by `prepareNewMatch`, which routes through the
GUI. A future driver must carry that.

**Not established by this run, and not claimed:**

- **Determinism.** One run, one position. Nothing here says a second run returns `(15,15)` again —
  and `InitialMoves.firstMove()` is known to use an unseeded `Random`, though that path was not
  taken here.
- **Strength.** No opponent, no game, no score. The move is legal, not good.
- **Rules equivalence with our engine.** No cross-check of state or legality against `TwixtState`
  was performed.
- **Anything at other plies, positions, or board sizes.** Fixed ply 3, one 24×24 position.

One position, one call, no game, no seeds.
