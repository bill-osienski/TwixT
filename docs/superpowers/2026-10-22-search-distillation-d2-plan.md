# Search distillation D2 — plan for Stages 2–5 (draft for approval)

**Date:** 2026-10-22

**Status:** DRAFT.
- **Approved:** Stage 0 (the offline trainer, `scripts/GPU/alphazero/search_distillation_d2.py`, with synthetic checks) and the Stage 1 measurement (card `2026-10-22-search-distillation-d2-stage1-card.md`).
- **Not approved:** Stages 2–5, promotion and pushing. Each needs its own approval and a frozen card.

**Where the numbers come from:** the cost figures below come from the proposal. Stage 1's measurement replaces the generation estimates before Stage 2 is put forward.

## 1. Mechanism

The experiment trains a student copy of `calib020_0001` to match the incumbent's own 1,600-simulation self-play visit counts, then judges the student by play at 400 simulations.

| Part | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Update surface | The encoder and the policy head |
| Value head, value adapter and every BatchNorm running statistic | Never updated. Training runs in evaluation mode, so normalization uses the frozen statistics exactly as inference does |
| Value anchor | A loss term pulling the student's value toward the teacher's (the incumbent's) value on the same positions. It is a safeguard and does not guarantee preservation, because the value head reads the encoder |
| Training passes | One offline step on a fixed corpus generated by the incumbent. No iteration |

This is neither ordinary continuation (#49) nor a capacity test (#66). Stage B (#54) stays closed. That run trained 519 policy-head parameters, judged them on proxies, and produced its labels with training-mode BatchNorm.

## 2. What is assumed, not measured

1. **The search advantage may not transfer to this protocol.** Stage A's mean pair score of 0.769 (1,600 vs 400 simulations) came from the earlier evaluation protocol:
   - the evaluation factory ran BatchNorm in training mode (ledger, Post-closure entry 3);
   - search used `search_with_root` with a fresh root and no root noise.

   Generation here differs:
   - the network runs in evaluation mode;
   - self-play adds root noise and reuses the tree.

   That the advantage carries over to corrected inference and to these targets is an assumption. Only Stage 5 tests it.
2. **Fitting the targets better may not mean stronger play.** Stage B's student fitted its targets better on average and still lost per-game comparisons.

## 3. Stage 2: generation (fixed schedule)

**Schedule.** 120 scheduled games: 12 phases of 10 games, a fresh 120-seed block, phase `p` = slots `10p … 10p+9`. Phases run in order through the serial capture driver at the Stage 1 card's configuration. Each phase is its own finalized capture directory.

**Split, fixed before generation:**
- The held-out games are every game whose slot is ≡ 9 (mod 10): one per phase, 12 in all.
- The other 108 games are training games.
- Held-out games are used only for Stage 3's stop checks. They are never trained on and never used for the strength comparison.

**Ceiling:** 12 hours of wall clock for the whole stage.

**Incomplete corpus.**
- A phase without `finalized.json` is discarded whole. No partial phase is used, and no phase is retried or replaced.
- Generation stops at the first capture failure or VOID, or at the ceiling.
- Training proceeds only if at least 6 phases finalized: 60 games, including 6 held-out. With fewer, Stage 2 counts as failed. Nothing is trained, and any next step needs a new plan.

**Repricing after Stage 1.** If Stage 1's measured seconds per ply, applied to its two games' mean length, project the 120 games above 12 hours, this schedule is revised and put forward again. It is not silently resized.

## 4. Stage 3: training (recipe frozen before training)

**Proposed recipe**, to be frozen unchanged in the Stage 3 card. It runs through `search_distillation_d2.train_student`.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Records | Every learner row of the training games: one per ply, no mirroring |
| Steps | `ceil(4 × N_train / 64)`, i.e. four passes |
| Batch | 64 |
| Optimizer | Adam, learning rate 1e-4 |
| Gradient clipping | Global-norm clip 1.0 |
| Loss | Policy cross-entropy; value-anchor weight 1.0; L2 1e-4 over the update surface |
| Draw-order seed | 0 |

**Stop rules** (each a STOP with no retune or rerun). All are measured on the held-out records against the teacher:
- any non-finite loss or gradient norm, or any check in `train_student` failing (surface, frozen tensors, teacher unchanged);
- held-out policy cross-entropy not below the teacher's;
- value drift beyond either limit, both on absolute differences, so that errors of opposite sign cannot cancel:
  - mean |v_student − v_teacher| > 0.05;
  - 95th percentile of |v_student − v_teacher| > 0.15.

  The maximum is reported, not gated. These limits are judgement calls (value range [−1, 1]) and are not derived from data.

**Output:** one student checkpoint, saved create-only. Its digest is recorded.

## 5. Stage 4: screen (gross regression only)

- **Games:** 32 colour-reversed pairs, both sides at 400 simulations, in evaluation mode, under the established evaluation-runner protocol, with fresh seeds.
- **Stop:** a mean pair score below 0.40. Above that, Stage 5 may be put forward.
- **Not a strength test:** the screen claims no strength.
- **Seeds:** its pairs are not part of Stage 5's sample.

## 6. Stage 5: strength comparison

**Unit.** One colour-reversed pair. Its score is the average of its two game scores, where a game scores 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw and 0 for a loss. A state cap counts as a draw. Pair scores therefore lie in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}.

**Size.** 509 fresh pairs (1,018 games), fixed in advance. There is no early look and no extension.

**Pass.** The Hoeffding 95% lower bound must exceed 0.5:

`mean − sqrt(ln 40 / (2 · 509)) > 0.5`, that is, an observed mean above **0.56020**.

Pairs are assumed independent. The size gives conservative 80% power at a true mean pair score of 0.60. Promotion after a pass is a separate decision.

## 9. Stage 1 result (2026-10-22, ledger entry 21)

Stage 1 completed in 124 s. The two games ran 52 and 54 plies, both decisive, at **1.1635 s per ply**. The live records equal the learner rows; 99 of 106 searches began with inherited child visits.

**Applying §3's repricing rule:**
- At these lengths the 120-game schedule projects to about 2.1 h.
- If every game reached the 280-ply cap, it would take about 10.9 h. Both figures are under the 12-hour ceiling, so the schedule stands.
- This assumes the per-ply time holds in long games, which was not measured beyond ply 54.

**Corpus size.** At these lengths the corpus would hold about 6,400 positions, not the proposal's ~10,000. Two games cannot estimate the length distribution, so the corpus size is uncertain too.

**Open for Stage 2 approval:** keep 120 games, or use the time headroom for more games. That would be a revision to this draft, decided before Stage 2 is approved; it must not be decided after seeing data.

## 10. Stage 2 terms as approved (2026-10-22). These replace §3 where the two differ.

- **Approved:** 120 games, not 240. The ~10,000-position figure was an estimate, not a requirement, and timing headroom alone does not justify doubling a feasibility corpus.
- **Configuration and split:** 12 ordered phases of 10 at Stage 1's exact configuration. Slot 9 of each phase is held out, giving 108 training and 12 held-out games.
- **Ceiling:** 12 hours in all, including capture and validation.
- **Stops:** the stage stops on the first capture failure or timeout, with no retry, replacement or top-up.
- **Eligibility: all 12 phases must complete before the corpus is eligible for training.** This replaces §3's six-phase fallback. If the stage stops, incomplete and completed phases are kept as diagnostics.
- **Exclusion:** the two Stage 1 games stay excluded.
- **Frozen card:** `2026-10-22-search-distillation-d2-stage2-card.md`.
- **Timing:** the 2.1-hour estimate is a projection from two games, and 10.9 hours is not a demonstrated worst-case bound.
- **Still unapproved:** training and Stages 4–5.
